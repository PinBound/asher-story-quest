# Asher's Story Quest: The Roadmap

**Student:** Asher Scardina, age 8 (an advanced writer)
**Course:** 22 missions in 3 lands, covering 3rd → 4th → 5th grade narrative-writing skills
**Pace:** 2–3 short sessions a week, about **7 months** (December 2026 → late June 2027)
**Where it lives:** the Asher's Story Quest app on his iPad (progress saves on that device)

---

## 1. The big picture

| Land | Grade level | Standard | Missions | About how long | Target dates | He finishes able to… |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Apprentice Village** | 3rd | W.3.3 | 1–7 + Boss 1 | 9–10 weeks | Nov 30, 2026 → mid-Feb 2027 | Write a complete story with a hero, setting, ordered events, dialogue, shown feelings and a real ending |
| **Dragon Mountains** | 4th | W.4.3 | 9–14 + Boss 2 | 8–9 weeks | mid-Feb → mid-Apr 2027 | Hook readers, use the senses and power words, vary transitions, build rising action to a climax, and write endings that grow out of the story |
| **Storm Castle** | 5th | W.5.3 | 16–21 + Final Boss | 9–10 weeks | mid-Apr → late June 2027 | Control pacing and point of view, write purposeful dialogue, use flashbacks, revise, edit and publish |

**Why this order matters:** each grade's standard builds on the one before. 3rd grade sets up the basic story parts. 4th adds detail, transitions and endings that follow from events. 5th adds pacing and more complex transitions. The app locks each mission until the one before it is done, so he can't learn 5th-grade skills before 3rd-grade ones. Being advanced means he may move faster, not skip steps.

**The dates are a guide, not a deadline.** If he's flying, go faster. If a mission is hard or he's having fun with it, repeat it with a new prompt. The goal is a kid who loves writing, not one who finished on time.

## 2. A normal week

| Day | What happens | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Session 1** (e.g., Monday) | Open the next mission. **Learn → Example → Play** together. Pick a prompt. | 15–20 min |
| **Session 2** (e.g., Wednesday) | **Write** the mission assignment with the 15-minute sprint timer, then go through the checklist. | 20–30 min |
| **Session 3** (e.g., Saturday) | **Share time.** He reads his piece to you or family. You give "two stars and a wish." He taps **Complete mission!** | 10–15 min |
| **Bonus (optional)** | Free write from the **Story Spinner** or a **story starter card**, with no rules and no checklist. | Any time |

**Boss weeks** take 1–2 weeks: plan in one session, draft over 1–2 sessions, check and revise, then a "big reading" for the family.

### Inside one session (the writer's workshop routine)
1. **Connect (1 min):** "Last time you learned ___. Today Ember's teaching ___."
2. **Learn + Example (5–8 min):** read both out loud. Ask: *"What did that writer do that we could steal?"*
3. **Play (5 min):** he does the game alone, and you only help if he asks.
4. **Write (15–20 min):** he works independently. Stay nearby but don't hover.
5. **Share (5 min):** this is the most important part, so never skip it.

### "Two stars and a wish" (how to give feedback)
- ⭐ **Star 1:** something specific he did well. *"The smoke coming out of the dragon's nose totally showed he was angry!"*
- ⭐ **Star 2:** another specific thing, ideally tied to this mission's skill.
- 🌠 **Wish:** one thing to try next time, also tied to this mission's skill. Just one.
- **Don't correct spelling and punctuation** until Mission 21 (Polish & Publish), unless he asks. Corrections too early teach him that writing means getting graded.

| Week | Dates (approximate) | Mission | Skill | What Asher makes | Win-condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | Apr 12 – 18 | **16. Speed Control** | Pacing | The big moment of an old story, rewritten in slow motion | One second now lasts a full paragraph, and boring parts get one sentence |

## 4. How to tell it's working

Keep a **portfolio folder**, paper or a Notes folder, with one piece from each mission. Every land boss is a checkpoint: lay his Boss 1, Boss 2 and Final Boss stories side by side.

| Checkpoint | Look for this growth |
|---|---|
| **After Boss 1** | A clear beginning, middle and end. Dialogue with quotation marks. At least one "showing" moment. An ending that actually ends. |
| **After Boss 2** | An opening sentence that grabs. Details you can see, hear and smell. Fewer "then"s and "very"s. A clear exciting peak. |
| **After the Final Boss** | Big moments slowed down. A consistent narrator. Dialogue that reveals character. He revises without being asked. |

**Compare his first Mac Beth story with his revised Mac Beth (Mission 20).** That before-and-after is the clearest proof of growth, and kids love seeing it.

## 5. Adjusting the pace

**If he's racing ahead**
- Let him do 2 missions a week, but **keep the Share step**.
- Add a **challenge**: "Do the mission's skill in a story told by the villain," or "Write it in under 100 words."
- Use the **Unlock all** switch in Parent corner *only* for review. Order still matters for new skills.
- Bigger stretch: a "series": every Boss story continues the same characters.

**If he's stuck or frustrated**
- Repeat the mission with a **different prompt**. Repeating is practice, not failure.
- Let him **tell the story out loud first** while you type or write it down, then he revises it himself.
- Shorten the writing: half a page is fine.
- Go back to the **Spinner** for a just-for-fun week.

**If he loses interest**
- Let him write in his favorite worlds: block-built games, storm islands, sports, animals. The Game Worlds and Sports spinner settings exist for exactly this.
- Swap the writing for **making a comic** for one mission. Panels and speech bubbles still teach sequence and dialogue.
- Give him a real audience: a grandparent who "orders" a new story every month.
